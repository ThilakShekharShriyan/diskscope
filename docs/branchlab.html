02 · Semantics

What can a second exec change?

One exec creates files and folders; a fresh exec then tries ten everyday operations on them — once as-is, once after archil checkout of the folder. Archil's default exec mount is shared mode, where deleting needs a delegation on the parent.

03 · Visibility

When does a long-lived reader see a change?

A reader exec primes its caches, then polls; a separate exec creates and overwrites files. Each access path has its own directory, so probes can't refresh each other. Archil's guide says a new file takes about 30 s to appear on another machine; this shows what that window is measured from.

07 · Limits

What this does and doesn't show

Steps 01, 04 and 05 use a fresh microVM per step, whose caches start empty — fresh-execution visibility. Section 03 is the long-lived-reader case, with its own caveats: n = 6, one region, wall clocks of two microVMs.

Archil commits a writing exec at exit (~70 ms), so a fresh-VM read after write-fsync can't separate fsync durability from that commit. No host failure is injected.

Correctness steps use journal_mode=TRUNCATE and clean up with a conditional mount, because of what section 02 shows. Full repros: ARCHIL-FINDINGS.md.

“≤31% fail” is the 95% upper bound on the failure rate after 0 failures in 8 trials: passing every trial is evidence, not proof. Writers never share a file. No disks, branches or checkpoints are created.
